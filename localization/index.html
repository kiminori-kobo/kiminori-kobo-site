Japanese documentation & support

For vendors of tools for developers, researchers and IT administrators · Tokyo

Kiminori Kobo is a sole proprietorship in Tokyo, run by a software engineer. We help small software vendors serve users in Japan by writing and maintaining the Japanese version of their documentation, and by answering Japanese users' questions in Japanese.

Contact us

Who this is for

  • Vendors of developer tools, research software (for example electronic lab notebooks and data or statistics tools) and IT administration tools
  • Small teams without Japanese-speaking staff, whose documentation is already in English and perhaps other languages, but not in Japanese
  • Documentation hosted on a platform such as HelpDocs, GitBook, ReadMe, Docusaurus or Mintlify, or kept in a Git repository

What we provide

  • A Japanese version of your documentation — starting with the pages your users read most, then extending to the rest.
  • Updates with each release — when the English pages change, the Japanese pages are updated to match.
  • First-line support in Japanese — we answer Japanese users' questions by email. Anything we can't solve goes to you in English, with a short summary and the steps to reproduce.
  • A managed glossary — an English–Japanese list of your product terms and on-screen labels, agreed with you and kept in sync, so the same word is translated the same way on every page.

How it works

  1. Get the source — we start from your documentation as it is: an export from your documentation platform, your docs repository, or the published pages.
  2. Build the glossary — we list your product terms and UI labels with proposed Japanese, and agree them with you before translating. On-screen labels that stay in English in your product stay in English in the Japanese pages, with a Japanese explanation.
  3. AI draft — a first draft is produced with AI translation, using the glossary.
  4. Engineer review — a software engineer checks every page against the English source: meaning, terminology, UI labels, links, code samples, and whether the steps match how the product actually behaves.
  5. Your approval — you review the Japanese pages (or tell us who should) and approve them.
  6. Publish on your platform — the Japanese pages go live on your own documentation site, for example as a Japanese language version in your documentation platform or a Japanese locale in your docs repository. We don't host copies of your documentation elsewhere.

Published only in the form you approve. No translation of your documentation is published without your approval, and only in the form and place you approve. We don't use your name or logo without your written permission.

Working with us

  • We work in writing: email, shared documents, and pull requests or the review tools of your documentation platform.
  • Before starting, we read your terms and licences for anything they say about translation, redistribution, trademarks and resale, and work within them.
